THE AUTHOR

Bradley Hobbs

Sometimes the stories that matter most are the ones we are willing to tell with honesty.

I wrote From the Bottom to Grace because some stories cannot be understood by looking only at where someone has been.

They have to be understood by looking at who they are becoming.

This story is personal to me because the woman at the center of it is my cousin. I have known her for most of my life. I saw her before addiction became part of her story, and I have watched the effects that addiction can have on a person and on an entire family.

WHY I WROTE THIS

I did not want to write another story that reduced a person to addiction.

Addiction can become such a powerful part of the way people see someone that they forget there was a person there before the addiction ever arrived.

I wanted to tell a different kind of story.

I wanted to tell the story of the person underneath the struggle.

I wanted to remember the woman I knew before addiction became part of her life, while also being honest about what addiction did.

Most of all, I wanted to write about what God could still see when everyone else could only see the brokenness.

WHAT GOD SAW

He saw a worshipper.

He saw someone worth restoring.

That truth changed the way I looked at her story.

It also changed the way I think about people who are struggling.

A person's worst season should never become the only thing we know about them.

A FAMILY STORY

This book is also about family.

I have watched members of my family experience the devastation that addiction can bring. Some have walked through it and found another way. Some are still struggling.

That is why I believe stories like this matter.

Sometimes someone needs to see that another person made it out before they can believe there is a way out for them.

FAITH AND GRACE

At the heart of this story is grace.

Not grace that ignores what happened, but grace that sees the truth and still believes restoration is possible.

God does not have to pretend our brokenness never happened in order to redeem us from it.

He can take the very places where we were wounded, trapped, and broken and begin writing something different there.

The past may explain where someone has been.

It does not have to determine where they are going.

MY HOPE FOR THIS STORY

My hope is that anyone who reads these pages will look beyond the label.

Look beyond the addiction.

Look beyond the mistakes.

Look beyond the years that may have been lost.

There is still a person there.

There is still a story being written.

And where God is involved, the story is not finished simply because the darkest chapter has already been written.

I believe her story is a testimony that surrender does not have to end in captivity.

Surrender can become worship.

Addiction does not get the final word.

Grace does.
