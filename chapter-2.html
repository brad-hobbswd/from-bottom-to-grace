Chapter Two

When Surrender Became Captivity

What began as an escape from pain became a place of captivity, but even there, God could still see the worshipper underneath it.

Addiction can begin by promising exactly what a hurting person thinks they need. It can offer an escape from pain, a moment of peace, or a way to quiet the things that are difficult to face. What begins as something that seems to provide relief can eventually become the very thing that controls a person's life.

That is what happened to her.

The thing she had turned to for relief eventually became the thing she could not easily turn away from. What had once seemed to offer an escape began taking more and more from her until it was no longer simply something she was choosing. It had become something that was holding her.

What God saw

When I look back on her story, though, I believe God saw something in her that even she could not see.

Beneath the addiction

He saw a worshipper.

She knew how to surrender.

She simply had not yet understood what that surrender was supposed to become.

That may sound strange when talking about someone who was struggling with addiction, but I believe it is one of the most important things about her story.

She knew how to surrender. She knew how to give herself completely to something. The tragedy was that addiction found that part of her before she understood what that surrender was supposed to become.

She bowed to something that could never love her back.

She gave herself to something that could only take.

And yet God knew that the same woman who could surrender herself so completely to something destructive could one day surrender herself completely to Him.

Grace was still working

God was not finished with her.

He knew what was underneath the addiction. He knew the person she was beneath the brokenness. He knew that there was still a worshipper there, even when her life did not look like worship at all.

Perhaps that is one of the greatest mysteries of God's grace. He can see what is still alive in us when everything around us seems to suggest that it is gone.

Two different desires

Her addiction wanted more of her.

God wanted her heart.

The thing that held her captive was slowly taking her life, but God was calling her toward the One who could restore it.

Her story also became a reflection of what was happening around her family. Some of her brothers and sisters have taken similar steps, and some are still struggling in those places.

They may not recognize themselves in her story yet, but perhaps one day they will. Perhaps they will look at what God has done in her life and realize that the road they are walking does not have to be the road they remain on.

The turning point

Because she turned around.

And that changed everything.
