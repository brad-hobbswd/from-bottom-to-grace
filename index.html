The Story Behind the Story

She was always more than her addiction.

She is my cousin, but that word alone cannot explain why her story means so much to me. I have known her for most of my life. Because she is older than all of her brothers and sisters, I had the opportunity to watch much of her life unfold.

I saw the woman she was before addiction became part of her story. I saw her family. I saw the struggles. I saw the things that happened along the way that many people outside our family could never fully understand.

I have also watched some of her brothers and sisters take steps down roads that look much like the one she once walked. Some are still there, and some may not even realize how far they have traveled.

That is why her story cannot simply be reduced to the word addiction.

Before the struggle

There was always something in her that wanted God.

She was raised in the Baptist faith. That was the faith she knew, and she believed what she had been taught. She attended church, worshipped, prayed, and experienced services where people cried out to God.

She was also a free spirit. There was something in her that wanted more than simply hearing about God. She wanted to experience Him. She wanted to know that He was real.

Sometimes her cry came through fear. Sometimes it came through desperation. But beneath those cries was a person searching for relief without yet understanding where that relief would ultimately come from.

She had the heart of a worshipper. She simply had not yet discovered who was worthy of that worship.

What changed

The thing that held her captive was killing her.

Addiction can begin by offering exactly what a hurting person thinks they need. It can offer escape, comfort, or temporary relief. But what begins as a place to hide can eventually become the thing that controls a person's life.

That is what happened to her. The thing she turned toward for relief eventually became the thing she could not easily turn away from.

But God saw something underneath all of it.

He saw a worshipper.

She knew how to surrender. She knew how to give herself completely to something. The tragedy was that addiction found that part of her before she understood what that surrender was meant to become.

God knew that if He could turn her around, the same woman who had surrendered herself to something destructive could surrender herself completely to Him.

The turning point

She did not stop being a worshipper.

As she grew older, her understanding of God began to change. The faith she had grown up with became something she began to examine personally, and she eventually embraced the apostolic doctrine she now believes.

Her faith became more than something she had inherited. It became personal.

The God she had cried out to in fear and desperation was not a distant God. He had been there all along.

She did not lose her ability to surrender. She found the right place to surrender it.

The difference was not her ability to surrender. The difference was who she surrendered to.

From the bottom to grace

What once held her captive
no longer defines her.

Her story is not simply a story about addiction. It is a story about surrender, redemption, grace, and a God who never stopped reaching for her.

She is still a worshipper.

She simply found the One worthy of her worship.
